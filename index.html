Independent Research

News-Conditioned Modeling of Firm Characteristics for Stock Return Prediction

Sep 2026 – Present
Independent Undergraduate Research — Georgia Tech

Financial news breaks daily; the accounting characteristics that describe a firm’s fundamentals only get refreshed on a quarterly or annual disclosure cycle. This project tests whether news should close that gap — updating a characteristic’s value between disclosures (State Updating) and/or changing how predictive that characteristic is, independent of its value (Effectiveness Modulation) — extending the Gu-Kelly-Xiu 94-characteristic empirical asset-pricing framework beyond its static, periodic updates.

3.7MCRSP-merged panel rows
0.14%out-of-sample R² baseline, matching published GKX benchmarks
2data-leakage bugs caught & fixed
30 yrscoefficient-stability window, 1987–2016
  • Built a leakage-safe, expanding-window ML pipeline (Ridge, Lasso, Elastic Net, Gradient Boosting) over the panel, with a strict time-series train/validate/test split — always tested on future data, never shuffled.
  • Established a correctly measured floor before testing the news hypothesis: out-of-sample R² baselines (up to 0.14%, pooled 1987–2016) matching the published Gu-Kelly-Xiu benchmarks.
  • Caught and fixed two data-leakage bugs during development — an unconverted annualized T-bill rate, and a risk-free-rate column that had leaked into the feature matrix.
  • Validated the pipeline with a 30-year coefficient-stability diagnostic (1987–2016) that recovers well-known equity anomalies — momentum, reversal, size, value — with consistent sign across three decades.

Closest prior work — Patton & Verardo (2012), “Does Beta Move with News?” — tests a single channel on a single characteristic with a causal event-study design. This project tests both channels across the full 94-characteristic panel with a predictive, out-of-sample framework.

Current stage: baseline and diagnostics complete. Next: building the news layer and testing both hypotheses.
